Your platform says 4x. How much of that has to be real for the ads to pay? Find the number before you scale or cut.

Contribution margin is what is left of each sale after product cost, shipping, fees and returns, before ads. Incremental means sales that would not have happened without the ads. Nobody knows that share without a test, so the verdict does not depend on your guess.

Reported ROAS counts every sale the platform can link to an ad, including people who would have bought anyway. Retargeting and brand search usually overstate the most, and cold prospecting least, but by how much is for a test to say.

How it works

The arithmetic
breakeven ROAS = 1 / contribution margin incremental ROAS = reported ROAS x incremental share profit per $1 = incremental ROAS x margin - 1 share needed to break even = 1 / (reported ROAS x margin)

Nothing here is estimated. It only turns your reported ROAS and margin into the one number that matters: how much of the reported result has to be real for the ads to pay.

The verdict

losing money: even if every reported sale is incremental, ROAS is below breakeven.

fragile: 75% or more of reported sales would need to be incremental.

depends on incrementality: between 40% and 75% needs to be incremental. This is where a holdout test earns its cost.

comfortable: under 40% needs to be incremental. The cut-offs are this tool's choice.

Reported vs real ROAS in 2026

Agency and vendor write-ups describe Meta changing its attribution reporting in 2026, including which actions count as click-through and engaged-view conversions, and advertisers seeing reported ROAS move with no change in sales. I could not confirm the details against Meta's own documentation, and the write-ups disagree on dates and direction. Compare periods that use the same attribution setting, trust sales in your own system over any platform's total, and check Meta's documentation before relying on a specific change.

Read this before you cut a budget on this

This does not measure incrementality. It tells you how much you need. A holdout or geo test tells you how much you have.

Margin is the soft spot. Include shipping, returns, payment fees and discounts. A margin that is too high flatters every row.

New customers are worth more than one sale. If the ads bring repeat buyers, one-sale profit understates them. Use a margin that includes expected repeat value if you can defend it.
